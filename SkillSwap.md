# **SkillSwap**

## **Product Requirements Document (PRD)**

**Tagline:** *Your Skills Are Currency.*

**Product:** SkillSwap  
**Product Type:** Global peer-to-peer skill exchange marketplace  
**Primary Experience:** Online, one-on-one live learning  
**Status:** Product definition / MVP planning

---

# **1\. Product Overview**

SkillSwap is a global peer-to-peer learning marketplace where people can exchange knowledge, skills, experience, and time.

Users can tell SkillSwap:

> **“This is what I can teach.”**  
> **“This is what I want to learn.”**

SkillSwap then helps them discover, match, connect, and learn from the right people.

Unlike traditional learning platforms, SkillSwap is not primarily based on pre-recorded courses.

Unlike freelance marketplaces, users are not primarily selling services.

Unlike social networks, the platform is structured around **learning and skill exchange**.

The core experience is:

**Discover → Match → Connect → Exchange → Review**

---

# **2\. Vision**

To create a world where anyone can turn their knowledge and skills into value.

SkillSwap's long-term vision is to make knowledge, experience, and time exchangeable value, allowing people around the world to teach what they know and learn what they need.

---

# **3\. Problem**

Millions of people possess useful skills but have no easy way to exchange them.

At the same time:

* Courses can be expensive.  
* Finding the right person to learn from can be difficult.  
* Social media is not designed for structured skill exchange.  
* Freelance platforms focus primarily on selling services.  
* Traditional learning platforms often separate teachers from learners.  
* People with practical experience may have valuable knowledge without formal qualifications.  
* People often want to learn from another person rather than consume another recorded course.

SkillSwap addresses this gap by creating a structured marketplace for **person-to-person learning and skill exchange**.

---

# **4\. Account Types and Target Users**

SkillSwap has two account types:

### **1\. User**

Every standard account can both teach and learn. A user can list skills they can teach, list skills they want to learn, and take either role in an exchange. Users do not create separate teacher and learner accounts or choose one role permanently.

“Teacher” and “learner” describe a user's role in a specific skill listing, session, or exchange. The same user may be a teacher in one exchange and a learner in another.

### **2\. Admin**

Admins are authorized SkillSwap operators who manage and support the marketplace. They review reports, disputes, appeals, and emergency cancellations; handle trust and safety actions; and resolve account, session, or credit issues. Admin accounts are for platform operations, not for participating as teachers or learners.

### **User audiences**

SkillSwap is designed for:

* Professionals  
* Students  
* Hobbyists  
* Beginners  
* Experienced practitioners  
* People changing careers  
* People developing new skills  
* People who want affordable learning  
* People who prefer exchanging knowledge instead of paying directly  
* People with practical skills but no formal qualifications  
* People who want to meet others around shared interests

In practice, every user can act as both a:

**Teacher \+ Learner**

# **19\. Direct SkillSwap**

Direct SkillSwap is the platform's signature exchange model.

Example:

**User A teaches Photography.**  
**User B teaches Excel.**

Instead of paying each other, they exchange skills.

The two users agree on:

* Skills  
* Session order  
* Schedule

The exchange contains two linked sessions.

---

# **20\. Direct-Swap Security**

Before a direct swap is confirmed, each user must have at least **half the Skill Credit value of the skill they want to learn**.

Example:

Photography \= 6 credits.

The learner deposits:

**3 credits**

Excel \= 4 credits.

The learner deposits:

**2 credits**

These credits are held as an accountability deposit.

They are not payment.

If both sides complete the exchange, their deposits remain theirs.

---

# **21\. Direct-Swap Cancellation**

If both users mutually cancel:

**Both deposits are returned.**

If one person cancels late and the other has not yet taught:

**The cancelling person's held credits go to the other person.**

If the other person has already taught:

**The cancelling person's held credits return to SkillSwap.**

SkillSwap then awards the person who completed their side the **full credit value of the skill they taught**.

---

# **22\. Direct-Swap Failure**

If one person completes their side and the other does not:

SkillSwap reviews the situation.

The person who successfully completed their side can receive the full credit value of the skill they taught.

Credits deducted from the responsible user return to SkillSwap.

---

# **23\. Direct-Swap Abuse**

Repeated abuse reduces Trust Level.

After **3 strikes**, direct-swap access is suspended for **1 month**.

During the restriction, the user can still:

* Teach  
* Learn with credits  
* Buy credits  
* Use credits  
* Message  
* Browse  
* Follow  
* Save  
* Participate in the community

Only direct swaps are restricted.

After the restriction, strikes remain and the user rebuilds trust through positive activity.

# **40\. Credit-Based Learning**

Teacher receives:

**4 credits**

SkillSwap retains:

**1 credit**

Anyone with useful knowledge can contribute.

### **3\. Two-sided identity**

Users are both learners and potential teachers.

### **4\. Direct SkillSwap**

Users can exchange one skill for another.

### **5\. Skill Credit economy**

Users can earn, buy, spend, and gift credits.

### **6\. Trust system**

The platform creates accountability without requiring everyone to have formal qualifications.

### **7\. Global accessibility**

Online sessions allow people to exchange skills across countries.

# **74\. Final Product Concept**

SkillSwap creates a new way of thinking about learning.

Instead of asking:

> “How much does this course cost?”

A user can ask:

> **“What can I teach in exchange for what I want to learn?”**

And if they do not have a suitable skill to exchange, they can use Skill Credits.

The fundamental idea is simple:

**Everyone knows something.**  
**Everyone can learn something.**  
**Everyone has something valuable to exchange.**
